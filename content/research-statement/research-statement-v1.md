# Research Statement — v1

**Eduardo Jose Yauri Luna**  
Research focus: Artificial Intelligence, Digital Transformation and Autonomous Systems for Business Processes

## Research motivation

Artificial intelligence is moving from isolated predictive or generative tools toward systems capable of planning, using tools, coordinating tasks and acting across business workflows. This transition creates opportunities for organisations to redesign operational processes, but it also introduces important research questions around reliability, governance, human oversight, accountability and measurable value.

My research interest is centred on how autonomous and semi-autonomous AI systems can be designed, integrated and evaluated in real organisational environments. I am particularly interested in small and medium-sized enterprises, where AI adoption often takes place under practical constraints including fragmented software, limited data infrastructure, reduced technical teams and a strong need to demonstrate operational value.

My professional background in digital strategy, web technologies, automation and software-based business systems motivates an applied research perspective. The objective is not simply to build functioning AI systems, but to study the conditions under which they become dependable, governable and useful components of organisational processes.

## Primary research direction

The main research direction concerns **autonomous AI agents for business processes**.

A central question is:

> How can autonomous and semi-autonomous AI agents be designed and evaluated so that they improve business processes while remaining reliable, traceable and governable?

This question can be decomposed into several research problems:

- How should tasks and decision authority be divided between humans and AI agents?
- Which architectural patterns best support observability, escalation and recovery from errors?
- How should business-agent performance be measured beyond simple task completion?
- What levels of autonomy are appropriate for different operational risks?
- How can organisations evaluate whether an AI-enabled process creates sustainable value rather than merely transferring complexity?
- Which organisational and technical factors determine successful adoption in SMEs?

## Human–AI collaboration and responsible automation

A second core area is the design of human–AI collaboration. Fully autonomous operation is not always desirable or appropriate. Many workflows require approval, contextual judgment, exception handling or accountability that remains with a human actor.

I am interested in models of calibrated autonomy in which AI systems can execute routine or bounded tasks while escalating uncertain, exceptional or high-impact decisions. Research in this area should consider trust, explainability, decision delegation, operational accountability and the relationship between automation quality and human supervision.

## Digital transformation in SMEs

AI adoption is also an organisational transformation problem. SMEs provide a particularly valuable research context because technological, financial and organisational constraints are visible and measurable.

Potential research questions include:

- Which capabilities are required before an organisation can successfully deploy agentic systems?
- How do integration quality, data quality and process maturity affect outcomes?
- What barriers prevent pilots from becoming stable operational systems?
- How can AI adoption maturity be measured in smaller organisations?
- What forms of governance are proportionate and practical for SMEs?

This line can support maturity models, comparative case studies and empirical analysis of AI adoption patterns.

## Generative search and AI-mediated discovery

A complementary research line examines the transition from traditional search toward generative and AI-mediated information discovery.

Generative systems increasingly select, summarize and synthesize information rather than simply ranking links. This changes how digital information becomes visible, how sources are selected and how entities establish machine-readable authority.

Potential research questions include:

- What characteristics make a source more likely to be retrieved or cited by generative systems?
- How do structured data and entity consistency affect discoverability?
- How stable are generative citation and source-selection patterns?
- How should visibility be measured when users receive synthesized answers rather than ranked result pages?
- What is the empirical relationship between traditional SEO signals and generative visibility?

The intention is to study these questions as information retrieval and measurement problems, rather than as a commercial optimization practice.

## Methodological approach

The planned research approach is applied and interdisciplinary. Depending on the specific research question, the methodology may combine:

- Design Science Research for the design and evaluation of technical artefacts
- Experimental evaluation of agent behaviour and workflow performance
- Software prototypes and research software
- Quantitative measurement of reliability, latency, cost, completion rate and error recovery
- Comparative organisational case studies
- Surveys and semi-structured interviews
- Mixed-method analysis
- Reproducible datasets, protocols and technical documentation

Where software is part of the research contribution, reproducibility and clear separation between implementation artefacts and empirical findings should be prioritised.

## Expected contribution

The long-term goal is to contribute frameworks, evidence and reusable technical artefacts that help organisations deploy AI systems more effectively and responsibly.

Potential contributions include:

1. Reference architectures for governable business AI agents.
2. Evaluation frameworks for autonomous and semi-autonomous workflows.
3. Models for calibrated human oversight and escalation.
4. AI adoption and readiness frameworks for SMEs.
5. Reproducible research software and datasets.
6. Empirical methods for measuring visibility in AI-mediated information discovery.

## Doctoral direction

A future doctoral project should concentrate the broader agenda into a clearly bounded primary question, most likely around the design and evaluation of autonomous AI agents in business processes. SME environments are a strong candidate context because they allow technical architecture, organisational adoption and measurable operational outcomes to be studied together.

The final doctoral proposal should be developed in dialogue with the selected research group and supervisor, ensuring alignment with their active research lines, available methodology and institutional requirements.
